Interpreting User Needs Through User Stories

Interpreting user needs begins with understanding what the user is trying to accomplish and why it matters. In Agile, this understanding is captured through user stories, which frame requirements from the user’s perspective. For example, in the SNHU Travel project, stories such as “As a traveler, I want to browse niche vacation packages so I can find unique travel experiences” helped clarify both the functionality and the motivation behind it.

User stories are powerful because they:

Keep the team focused on user value, not just technical tasks

Provide clear acceptance criteria that define “done”

Allow the Product Owner to prioritize work based on business goals

Enable Developers to break work into actionable tasks

Support iterative refinement as user needs evolve

By grounding development in user stories, the team ensures that every increment of work directly serves the user’s goals.

Approaching Program Development with Agile Practices

My approach to developing programs has become increasingly shaped by Agile principles. Instead of attempting to design an entire system upfront, I now focus on iterative, incremental development. Agile encourages delivering small slices of functionality, gathering feedback, and adapting quickly—an approach that proved essential when SNHU Travel shifted toward wellness‑focused travel mid‑project.

The Agile processes I plan to incorporate into future development work include:

Sprint Planning to set realistic goals and align the team

Daily Scrums to maintain transparency and momentum

Backlog Refinement to clarify requirements and ensure readiness

Sprint Reviews to gather stakeholder feedback early and often

Sprint Retrospectives to continuously improve team processes

These practices create a predictable rhythm of delivery and help teams respond effectively to changing requirements—something traditional waterfall approaches struggle with.

Being a Good Team Member in Software Development

Being a good team member in software development means contributing not only code, but also communication, collaboration, and accountability. Scrum emphasizes that the entire team shares responsibility for delivering value, regardless of role.

A good team member:

Communicates clearly and concisely

Shares progress, blockers, and dependencies

Helps refine user stories and acceptance criteria

Supports teammates when challenges arise

Participates actively in Scrum events

Upholds the Definition of Done and quality standards

Embraces feedback and continuous improvement

Throughout the SNHU Travel project, rotating roles reinforced how each accountability—Product Owner, Developer, and Scrum Master—depends on strong teamwork. Whether defining requirements, building features, or facilitating collaboration, success comes from working as a unified Scrum Team.
